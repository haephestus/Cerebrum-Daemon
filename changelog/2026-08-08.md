## 1. Learning profiles — two-layer (declared + inferred), confidence-gated blend, hybrid inference, write-time wiring

**What.** A learner is modelled as a small vector of 4 orthogonal axes
(abstraction↔concrete, breadth↔depth, pattern↔detail, worked-example↔discovery),
each a scalar in [-1, 1]. Two layers are stored SEPARATELY and never overwrite
each other: **declared** (what the user says — the Bayesian prior) and
**inferred** (an append-only evidence log — the evidence). The **effective**
profile blends them confidence-gated: cold start → declared dominates; as
behavioural evidence accumulates the inferred posterior grows and can override.
Inference is **hybrid** — deterministic structural signals now, with a
feature-flagged LLM-enrichment seam for later. Inference is wired **write-time**
at the two points behaviour changes: note analysis, and mastery recompute.

**Why.** Teaching should adapt to the individual (e.g. a pattern-first
"meta-learner" needs detail surfaced so the overall shape holds at the specific
scale). Nothing modelled this before (`user_inator.py` had no learning-style
concept). Two layers kept apart give transparency ("you said X, we observe Y"),
clean re-assertion, and drift audit; weighting toward inferred *but* gated by
evidence avoids both the cold-start problem and ignoring what the user
explicitly asked for.
